# Aurora Ridge Observatory

Aurora Ridge Observatory is a fictional research facility used for the CiteWise
demo corpus. It is located about 18 km northwest of the town of Lake Harrow, on
a ridge that is 2,140 m high. The site was chosen in the late 1970s for its dark
skies, stable air, and the fact that the ridge rises above the valley haze that
settles over Lake Harrow in summer.

The observatory is owned and operated by the Northern Sky Consortium, a
partnership of three fictional institutions: Alberon University, the Veyra
Institute, and Lake Harrow College. Its mission has three parts: time-domain
astronomy, follow-up of transiting exoplanet candidates, and public science
education. The 3.2 m Meridian Telescope saw first light in 2004 and remains the
facility's main instrument.

A permanent staff of 24 people runs the site. That includes 11 astronomers,
6 engineers, 3 instrument specialists, and 4 people in operations and outreach.
Additional observers visit from consortium universities for scheduled runs,
and graduate students often stay for two to three weeks at a time. The resident
team handles telescope operations, instrument maintenance, software, and the
data archive.

Public access is deliberately limited so that it does not interfere with
observing. The visitor gallery is open on Saturdays from May through September,
with tours at 11:00 and 14:00. Each tour is capped at 40 people and must be
reserved in advance; tours are free. Once a month, the observatory also hosts a
night program where visitors can look through the 0.6 m Lumen Telescope while
staff explain how the main telescope works. The gallery is closed during the
winter closure and during poor weather.

Aurora Ridge is not a large observatory by professional standards, but it has a
distinctive role. Its longitude and equipment make it useful for continuous
coverage of variable stars and for confirming transit signals found by survey
telescopes. The consortium describes the site as a place where "small
telescopes do careful work", and that phrase appears on the sign at the access
road.

The ridge itself is a protected area. Outdoor lighting within the observatory
fence is kept to the minimum needed for safety, and the surrounding county has
adopted lighting rules that the observatory helped draft. Those rules are
described in the outreach and dark-sky document in this corpus.
